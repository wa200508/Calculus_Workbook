## D1-001 — Polynomial differentiation by linearity

Independent termwise differentiation gives

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(4x^3-5x^2+7x-9)=12x^2-10x+7.
$$

Every coefficient is fixed, the derivative of the constant is zero, and the
polynomial is differentiable on the whole real line. To check the general
formula independently by increments, the difference quotient expands to

$$
12x^2-10x+7+(12x-5)h+4h^2,
$$

which tends to the same derivative for every fixed real $x$. The manuscript's
individual sign multiplications and exponent subtractions are correct. Its
one-point check gives $4h^2-5h+7\to7$ at zero and explicitly limits that check's
scope. All three hints are valid; no excluded input or exceptional coefficient
case occurs.

## D1-002 — Radicals and negative powers

On the required positive domain, writing the function as
$5x^{1/2}-3x^{-2}+2$ gives

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(f(x))=\frac{5}{2\sqrt{x}}+\frac6{x^3}.
$$

The positive reciprocal derivative follows from $(-3)(-2)=6$; both terms have
the correct exponents. At the stated input,
$5/(2\sqrt4)+6/4^3=5/4+3/32=43/32$, exactly. An independent root check
rationalizes $(\sqrt{x+h}-\sqrt{x})/h$ to
$1/(\sqrt{x+h}+\sqrt{x})\to1/(2\sqrt{x})$ for fixed $x>0$.
For the reciprocal term,

$$
\frac{-3/(x+h)^2+3/x^2}{h}
=\frac{3(2x+h)}{x^2(x+h)^2}\longrightarrow\frac6{x^3}.
$$

The root branch and the reciprocal both respect the given positive domain.
Zero is excluded by the original reciprocal; negative inputs cannot be used
as real inputs for the specified root. The hints, exact fraction arithmetic,
and monotonicity check in the solution agree with these facts.

## D1-003 — Differentiation with fixed parameters

With $a,b$ fixed, the independent increment quotient is exactly
$a(2x+h)+b$ and tends to $2ax+b$. This proves the result for every real input
and all real parameter values, including $a=0$, $b=0$, and the constant case
$a=b=0$. Substitution after differentiation gives
$2(2)(3)-1=11$. Holding $a,b$ fixed is essential: substituting $a(x)$ would
give a different problem with an additional dependence. The statement, all
three hints, and solution preserve the declared fixed parameters. The
specialized function $2x^2-x+4$ independently yields $4x-1$ and the same
evaluated slope. No branch or denominator excludes any real input.

## D1-004 — Cancellation and a removable singularity

Factorization gives $f(x)=x+3$ only for $x\ne3$. For every such input, choosing
$|h|<|x-3|/2$ ensures both increment values remain in the domain, and the
nonzero-increment quotient is identically one. The derivative is therefore
one on both connected components of the original domain.

At $x=3$, the original function has no value, so the original difference
quotient and derivative are undefined. The unique continuous extension has
value six there and agrees with $x+3$ on the whole line; its derivative at
three is one. This is a different function with a larger domain. The student
claim in the statement does not follow for the original function. The hints,
factorization, cancellation restriction, local-neighborhood argument, and
final distinction in the solution all correctly preserve the excluded point.

## D1-005 — Difference-quotient derivation

Expansion from the statement gives
$f(x+h)-f(x)=2xh+h^2+2h=h(2x+h+2)$. For nonzero $h$, division gives
$2x+h+2$, with limit $2x+2$. This derives the formula without using the power
rule, as requested. At $x=-1$ it gives zero, whereas the function value itself
is $-1$. The comparison with the power rule appears only as a subsequent
check in the solution.

All real increments are allowed by the original polynomial, and the limit
holds with $x$ fixed. The hints and each expansion, subtraction, cancellation,
and evaluation in the solution are correct. The warning against substituting
$x=-1$ before differentiating is also correct: the resulting constant happens
to have derivative zero, but that coincidence cannot determine the derivative
of the function before evaluation. No endpoint or exceptional input is hidden.

## D1-006 — Algebraic reduction before differentiation

On each component of $x\ne0$, divide the numerator termwise to obtain
$f(x)=2x^2-3+4/x$. Differentiation gives $4x-4/x^2$, whose value at two is
$8-1=7$. Independently, the original quotient rule gives numerator

$$
(6x^2-3)x-(2x^3-3x+4)=4x^3-4,
$$

and dividing by $x^2$ gives the same derivative. The original numerator tends
to four at zero, so there is a genuine pole there; no cancellation removes it.

The solution's difference quotient at two reduces to
$8+2h-2/(2+h)$, with limit seven, and its intermediate arithmetic is correct.
The value $f(2)$ also happens to equal seven, but the solution computes the
slope independently and does not use that coincidence as a justification.
The statement, hints, reductions, and checks all retain $x\ne0$.

## D2-001 — Differentiate a composition

Independent differentiation of the outer fourth power and the inner quadratic
gives $4(3x^2+1)^3(6x)=24x(3x^2+1)^3$. A separate full expansion gives

$$
f(x)=81x^8+108x^6+54x^4+12x^2+1,
$$

whose derivative is $648x^7+648x^5+216x^3+24x$, equal to the factored chain-rule
answer. The inner polynomial is defined everywhere; indeed it is at least one,
so there is no hidden root, logarithm, or division restriction.

The declared outer input is independent during its differentiation and is
correctly replaced by the inner function before multiplication. The hints and
all factors in the solution agree. Its increment expansion at zero has
coefficients $12,54,108,81$ and tends to zero after division, correctly
checking that input without claiming to prove every input.

## D2-002 — A polynomial times a sine

From the product increment identity, the derivative has one term from the
polynomial increment and one from the sine increment:
$2x\sin x+x^2\cos x$. In radians the sine derivative is $\cos x$; this angle
convention is stated explicitly. Both factors are smooth for every real input.
At $x=\pi$ the two terms evaluate to zero and $-\pi^2$, respectively.

The independent evaluated difference quotient uses
$f(\pi)=0$ and $\sin(\pi+h)=-\sin h$, giving
$-(\pi+h)^2\sin h/h\to-\pi^2$. This matches every sign and factor in the
manuscript's product-rule solution and trigonometric check. The three hints
preserve the unchanged factor in each product-rule term. No parameter or
excluded endpoint is missing.

## D2-003 — A quotient with an excluded input

Polynomial division independently gives
$f(x)=x+1+2/(x-1)$ on $x\ne1$. Its derivative is

$$
1-\frac{2}{(x-1)^2}
=\frac{x^2-2x-1}{(x-1)^2}.
$$

The quotient-rule numerator independently expands as
$2x(x-1)-(x^2+1)=x^2-2x-1$. At two the value is $-1$. At the excluded input

canceling a factor. Both open components of the stated domain admit the same
derivative formula. The hints, parentheses in the subtraction, denominator
square, evaluated arithmetic, and equivalent-expression check are all correct.

## D2-004 — An exponential of a quadratic

Differentiating the exponent first gives $4x$, hence the derivative is
$4x\exp(2x^2-3)$ for every real $x$. An independent logarithmic relation is
$\ln(f(x))=2x^2-3$, valid because $f(x)>0$. Differentiating this identity gives
$\frac{d}{dx}(f(x))/f(x)=4x$, agreeing with the chain-rule result.

At zero the derivative is zero, while $f(0)=\exp(-3)$ is positive. The solution
keeps these quantities distinct and multiplies the finite exponential by zero
correctly. Its symmetry/minimum checks agree with the derivative's sign. The
outer derivative is evaluated at the entire exponent, the factor four is
present, and all three hints agree. No real-domain limitation is needed.

## D2-005 — Logarithmic composition

The logarithm's argument is $1+x^2\ge1$, so the declared real domain is all of
$\mathbb R$ and the chain-rule denominator cannot vanish. Differentiation
gives $2x/(1+x^2)$; evaluation at one is exactly one. Independently,

$$
\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}
=\frac1h\ln\left(1+\frac{2xh+h^2}{1+x^2}\right)
\longrightarrow\frac{2x}{1+x^2},
$$

using $\ln(1+v)/v\to1$. This also handles $x=0$; the quotient's factor
$(2x+h)/(1+x^2)$ then tends to zero. The solution's sign and minimum claims
are valid. Its warning that a logarithm of a sum cannot be split into a sum
of logarithms is correct. The hints and the inner-input substitution retain
the required strict positivity.

## D2-006 — Square-root composition

Here $1+x^2\ge1$, so the chosen nonnegative square root is in fact strictly
positive and smooth at every real input. Rationalization independently yields

$$
\frac{\sqrt{1+(x+h)^2}-\sqrt{1+x^2}}{h}
=\frac{2x+h}{\sqrt{1+(x+h)^2}+\sqrt{1+x^2}}
\longrightarrow\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}.
$$

The derivative at zero is zero. The chain-rule factors, exponent subtraction,
and evaluation in the solution all match. Its separate defining-relation check
uses $(f(x))^2=1+x^2$ and divides by $2f(x)>0$; that division is valid on the
entire stated domain. No negative square-root branch or spurious absolute-value
substitution is introduced by the hints or solution.

## D2-007 — Nested trigonometric composition

The three independent chain-rule factors are $3(\sin(2x))^2$, $\cos(2x)$, and
two. Their product is $6(\sin(2x))^2\cos(2x)$, valid for all real angles in
radians. A separate trigonometric representation gives

$$
f(x)=\frac{3\sin(2x)-\sin(6x)}4,
\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}(f(x))=\frac32\bigl(\cos(2x)-\cos(6x)\bigr),
$$

equal to the product formula by angle identities. At $x=\pi/4$ the cosine
factor is zero, so the slope is zero although the function value is one.
Locally $f(\pi/4+h)=(\cos(2h))^3$, an even differentiable function of the
increment; its derivative at zero vanishes independently. The hints retain
the inner factor two, the solution's algebra and angle evaluations are exact,
and the special-input check has the correct scope. There are no excluded real
inputs or branch qualifications.
