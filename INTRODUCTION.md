# About this workbook

Knowing the rules of calculus and being able to carry a difficult calculation
through to completion are different accomplishments. A familiar derivative can
be hidden inside an unfamiliar expression. A promising substitution can fail at
a turning point. Several pages of correct algebra can still lead to a result
that is valid on only part of the original domain. These are the difficulties
this workbook is intended to exercise.

The reader is assumed to have studied calculus. The aim is sustained practice:
choosing a way into a problem, developing it far enough to see whether it works,
and accounting for the assumptions on which the result depends. Some solutions
become short after a useful reformulation. Others remain long. Both kinds belong
here. A complicated answer is acceptable when the calculation is sound and its
meaning is clear.

Notation provides the common language for that work. The opening chapter records
the conventions used throughout the book: what each quantity represents, which
variables it depends on, and precisely which operation is being performed. Full
function arguments and explicit derivative operators reduce the amount that has
to be reconstructed from context. The methods reference that follows collects
formulas and their conditions in one place, so a calculation can be resumed
without relying on perfect recall.

The main chapters contain mixed problems. A heading identifies the mathematical
setting, but the choice of method is part of the problem. The worked solutions
explain what made an approach promising, carry out the intermediate calculations,
and examine the places where that approach could have failed. Algebra appears
in dark blue so that a sign, coefficient, or cancellation can be checked without
losing the thread of the argument. Domain discussions are marked in amber;
supporting identities and approximations appear in purple. The labels remain
useful in a monochrome printout.

Each worked problem in the printed edition begins with its complete statement
and continues directly into the solution. The problem identifiers and numbered
steps make particular calculations easy to find again. Hints are collected in
a separate appendix for readers who want a smaller amount of help first. On the
website, the hints and solutions can be opened individually. Simpler reference
exercises are retained in the appendices when a local calculation needs a more
familiar example.

The purpose of the answers is to make independent work possible, including the
part of that work that involves becoming stuck. Consulting an identity, comparing
two representations, or returning to an earlier line is part of solving a
problem. Over time, repeated encounters with those decisions build a practical
sense of which approaches are likely to succeed and which conditions deserve
closer attention.
